Kingsley Ranch Braced for Sage, Receives Anatomy Charts; Sale Manager Unable to Say How Long Riding Lesson Lasted

COTTONWOOD RIDGE — When word reached the Cottonwood Café that Boone Kingsley had hired a massage therapist from Albuquerque for his horses, the counter prepared for incense, wind chimes, and a great deal of talk about energy. Jayda Fairchild arrived in mid-November with a folding table, a truck full of laminated anatomy charts, and one crystal, which rides in her cupholder and which she says was a gift.

Within the week, Midnight, Boone's twenty-three-year-old stallion, crossed the paddock like a horse half his age. Mr. Kingsley, who hired Ms. Fairchild on a friend's word, now says he is keeping her on his own. He said nothing further, which from Boone amounts to a parade.

Ms. Fairchild has also failed the ranch's horseshoe-and-chain gate latch, the traditional initiation for new hands, three separate ways. The figure was confirmed by Shane Kingsley, who is running the Kingsley Christmas Classic this December and noted the time of each attempt.

Mr. Kingsley is teaching her to ride on Sunny, Boone's gentlest mare, in exchange for bodywork on a back he prefers not to discuss. Both parties describe this as a trade. Sunny has declined to describe it at all.

Asked how long Tuesday's lesson ran, Mr. Kingsley, who can tell you to the minute when he last drove through town, said he wasn't sure. Asked again, he said about an hour. The lesson began at two. His truck left at a quarter to six.

Hawaiian Sweet Potatoes

Holiday Table Favourite · Serves 8–12

Ingredients • 6 sweet potatoes, washed and halved lengthwise (do not peel)
• ⅓ cup sugar
• ½ cup butter, softened
• 1 tsp cinnamon
• 1 can (8 oz) crushed pineapple
• ½ cup chopped pecans
• ⅓ cup packed brown sugar
• 2 Tbl butter, for dotting Directions 1. Boil the halved potatoes in water to cover until tender, about 30 minutes. Drain well.
2. Scoop out the pulp into a large bowl, keeping the shells intact.
3. Mash the pulp with the sugar, butter and cinnamon.
4. Drain the pineapple, reserving the liquid. Stir the pineapple and pecans into the potatoes.
5. Dip the empty shells in the reserved pineapple juice, then spoon the mixture back in.
6. Sprinkle with brown sugar and dot with butter. You can make it ahead to this point.
7. Bake at 350°F for 15–20 minutes, until heated through.

Thinking of making this?

👩‍🍳 Going to Try 👍 Liked It 👎 Disliked It

This is a holiday-table dish, the kind that turns up in a nine-by-thirteen and disappears first. Make it the day before and bake it while everyone is arguing about the seating. Jayda would tell you the pineapple is what makes it; Shane would want the pan out of the oven at the minute he said it would be, and he would be right, and nobody would thank him for it.

The Kingsleys of Cottonwood Ridge:

Hold Still, Cowboy

Book 9 in the Series

She fixes what hurts. He might be the reason she finally stays. Equine massage therapist Jayda Fairchild is good at two things: finding the pain nobody else can see, and leaving town before anyone gets close. Her contract at the Kingsley Ranch should be no different — ready the horses for the Christmas Classic, collect her pay, head south before the Montana snow traps her. Then she meets Shane Kingsley, who came home after a betrayal cost him the company he spent eight years building, and who has no intention of trusting anyone again. When his injured back lands him on her table, they strike a bargain: her healing hands for his riding lessons. It should keep things professional.

💌   A Note from JC   💌

From My Desk to Yours

"Shane spends this whole book insisting he is fine. He has a schedule, a spreadsheet, and a back that disagrees with both. What I loved writing was how long it took him to let somebody put a hand on the part that actually hurt — and how patient Jayda was about waiting for it. Some people need to be asked twice. Some need to be asked for eight years. Here is to whoever keeps asking."

— JC 🤍
